Blood Ketone Glossary

The language around blood ketones is easy to confuse — ketonemia, ketosis, ketonuria, and ketoacidosis are related but not interchangeable. This is the governed glossary: every term with its measurement layer, what it is not, a clinical-boundary note, and a link to its canonical reference. Search it, cite it, return to it.

ketonemia

blood measurement state

The presence or elevation of ketone bodies measurable in blood.

Not the same as: ketosis, ketonuria, ketoacidosis, keto diet

Boundary: Describes a measurement state, not a diagnosis, safety verdict, or treatment need.

Reference: /definition/

ketosis

metabolic process

A metabolic process in which the body increases production and use of ketone bodies for fuel, commonly discussed in fasting and nutritional contexts.

Not the same as: ketonemia, ketoacidosis

Boundary: A metabolic process is not automatically beneficial or harmful; context determines meaning.

Reference: /ketonemia-vs-ketosis/

ketonuria

urinary finding

The presence of ketone bodies detectable in urine, typically measured as acetoacetate on urine strips.

Not the same as: ketonemia, ketosis, ketoacidosis

Boundary: A urinary finding is not interchangeable with a blood ketone measurement.

Reference: /laboratory-context/

ketoacidosis

clinical syndrome

A serious clinical syndrome involving a broader clinical picture, not a blood ketone number alone.

Not the same as: ketonemia, ketosis, ketonuria

Boundary: This site explains the relationship but does not diagnose ketoacidosis. Clinical assessment is required.

Reference: /ketonemia-vs-ketoacidosis/

DKA

clinical syndrome

Diabetic ketoacidosis: a serious diabetes complication involving ketones and blood acidity, recognized through clinical assessment and laboratory context.

Not the same as: ketonemia, ketosis

Boundary: DKA must not be inferred from a ketone reading alone. Seek professional care for concerns.

Reference: /ketonemia-vs-ketoacidosis/

beta-hydroxybutyrate

blood ketone body

The predominant ketone body measured in blood at elevation and the primary analyte of blood ketone meters.

Boundary: A biochemical marker; its measurement does not by itself carry a clinical verdict.

Reference: /beta-hydroxybutyrate/

acetoacetate

ketone body

A ketone body commonly measured in urine by colorimetric strips; a different fraction from blood BHB.

Boundary: Urine AcAc can lag or appear discordant relative to blood BHB and is not interchangeable with it.

Reference: /laboratory-context/

acetone

breath ketone body

A volatile ketone body detectable in breath, measured as a proxy signal by breath analyzers.

Boundary: A proxy measurement; not interchangeable with blood BHB or urine AcAc.

Reference: /laboratory-context/

blood ketones

measurement layer

Ketone bodies measured in blood, predominantly beta-hydroxybutyrate, reported by meters or laboratory analyzers.

Boundary: A blood ketone number is not context-free and does not on its own indicate a clinical state.

Reference: /blood-ketones/

urine ketones

measurement layer

Ketone bodies detected in urine, typically acetoacetate, measured by strip color change.

Boundary: A urinary measurement layer distinct from blood measurement; not interchangeable.

Reference: /laboratory-context/

breath acetone

measurement layer

Acetone measured in exhaled breath as a proxy signal for ketone metabolism.

Boundary: A proxy signal; measurement precision and meaning differ from blood measurement.

Reference: /laboratory-context/

SGLT2 inhibitor context

medication context

A medication context in which ketone elevation can occur at blood glucose levels that would not typically prompt concern, documented as euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis risk.

Boundary: This site documents the phenomenon; it does not assess individual medication risk.

Reference: /ketonemia-vs-ketoacidosis/

clinical boundary

governance

The formal scope limit separating educational reference from clinical practice on Ketonemia.com.

Boundary: The site operates on the educational side of this boundary only.

Reference: /clinical-boundary/

signal map

interface

The conceptual interface organizing blood ketone signals across state zones (KSS), context dimensions, and interpretation boundaries.

Boundary: Classifies interpretation layers, not people; it is not a calculator, symptom checker, or triage tool.

Reference: /signal-map/
