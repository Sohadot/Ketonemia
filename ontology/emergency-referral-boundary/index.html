Emergency Referral Boundary

The emergency referral boundary is not treatment advice. It is a boundary class for language that points toward urgent or emergency medical evaluation where cited guidance supports it — and it must never ask a reader to self-triage.

Governing Rule (KSO)

Not treatment advice. A boundary class for language that points toward urgent or emergency medical evaluation where source guidance supports it. Must not ask users to self-triage.

What This Class Is

The Emergency Referral Boundary is the highest boundary class in the ontology. It governs language that points toward urgent or emergency medical evaluation, used only where cited source guidance supports it. It exists so that, at the edge of the reference surface, the site can direct a reader toward professional care rather than attempt to manage a situation itself.

The ontology rule is strict: this class is not treatment advice, and it must not ask users to self-triage. It points outward to professional evaluation; it does not grade a reader’s condition or tell them how urgent their own situation is.

What It Is Not

The boundary is defined by two refusals:

  • Not treatment advice. Pointing toward emergency evaluation is not instruction on what to do medically. It is a referral of the question to professionals.
  • Not self-triage. This class never asks a reader to judge their own severity or decide whether they qualify as an emergency. That determination belongs to a professional.

Relationship to the KSS Standard

This class aligns with the KSS K5 — Emergency Referral Boundary: an urgent-care language zone used only when cited guidance supports emergency referral. K5 is a reference boundary label describing where language points outward to professional evaluation — not a threshold a reader applies to themselves. See the Signal Map.

Boundary Language

Points to care; never self-triage. This boundary class directs toward professional or emergency evaluation where cited guidance supports it. It is not treatment advice, and it does not ask you to judge your own severity. If you think you may need urgent care — particularly with symptoms such as vomiting, abdominal pain, rapid breathing, or confusion — contact a qualified healthcare professional or seek emergency care. See the clinical boundary.

Audience Notes

Clinical educators This is the outermost boundary class. Its entire function is to hand off to professional care without triaging, instructing, or grading — the cleanest expression of the site’s scope limit.
Public readers At this edge, the site’s only role is to point you toward professional care. It cannot tell you how urgent your situation is. If you are worried you may need urgent care, seek it.
