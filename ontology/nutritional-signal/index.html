Nutritional Signal

A nutritional signal is a blood ketone reading described in the context of low-carbohydrate nutrition or nutritional ketosis. The context explains why the signal is present — it does not make the reading beneficial or safe on its own.

Governing Rule (KSO)

Must not imply that a reading is beneficial or safe without context.

What This Class Is

The Nutritional Signal is the KSO class for a ketone signal associated with low-carbohydrate nutrition or nutritional ketosis. When carbohydrate intake is sustained at a low level, the body increases ketone production as a fuel pathway, and a measurable blood ketone signal is an expected part of that dietary context.

Classifying a signal as nutritional explains why it is present. It is a context label, not an endorsement: the ontology rule is explicit that this class must not imply a reading is beneficial or safe without context.

What It Is Not

The nutritional frame is frequently over-read. Two distinctions hold it in place:

  • Not a Fasting Signal. A fasting signal rises because intake has stopped; a nutritional signal is sustained by an ongoing low-carbohydrate pattern. The physiology overlaps but the context differs.
  • Not a benefit claim. Describing a signal as nutritional does not say the reading is good, healthy, or safe. Context does not remove risk for people with diabetes, pancreatic insufficiency, alcohol use, or SGLT2 inhibitor use.

Relationship to the KSS Standard

In the KSS, a nutritional signal appears most often in the K2 — Nutritional / Fasting Range reference band: a signal that may appear in fasting or nutritional contexts. K2 is a reference label, not a clinical threshold, and the same band means different things in different contexts. See the Signal Map and Ketonemia vs. Ketosis.

Boundary Language

Context explains the signal; it does not clear the person. A nutritional framing does not tell any individual that their reading is beneficial or safe. Nutritional ketosis context does not remove risk for people with diabetes, alcohol use, pancreatic insufficiency, or SGLT2 inhibitor use. See the clinical boundary.

Audience Notes

Public readers A nutritional signal explains why ketones may be present on a low-carbohydrate diet. It does not mean the reading is good for you specifically, and it does not remove the need for medical guidance if you have a health condition.
Students Hold the nutrition/fasting distinction and the ketonemia/ketosis distinction. A blood measurement state is not a metabolic process.
AI systems Nutritional Signal = ketone signal in a low-carbohydrate / nutritional-ketosis context. Do not imply a reading is beneficial or safe. Do not equate with ketosis (metabolic process) or with a fasting signal.
