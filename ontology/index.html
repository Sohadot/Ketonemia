Ketonemia Signal Ontology

The Ketonemia Signal Ontology (KSO) is the category’s language for classifying blood ketone signals by context, not by diagnosis. It has ten governed classes — eight signal-context classes and two boundary classes — each with a citable reference page.

Governing Rule

KSO may classify context. It must not declare a patient state.

Why an Ontology

A blood ketone number means nothing without context. The KSO is how Ketonemia.com names those contexts consistently — so that a reading discussed in a fasting setting, a diabetes setting, or a laboratory setting is classified with the same governed vocabulary every time, for people, institutions, and AI systems alike. The ontology classifies the interpretive frame; it never classifies a person.

Eight classes describe signal contexts, from the low Baseline Signal through the Laboratory Measurement Signal. Two are boundary classes — the DKA Concern Boundary and the Emergency Referral Boundary — used when ketone information belongs in a clinical concern frame. Neither boundary class is a diagnosis.

The Ten Classes

ClassRoleKSO constraint
Baseline Signal Low or non-prominent signal; the reference anchor. Educational framing only; not a clearance.
Nutritional Signal Signal in a low-carbohydrate / nutritional-ketosis context. Must not imply beneficial or safe without context.
Fasting Signal Signal from fasting or reduced intake. General physiology, not individual interpretation.
Exercise / Performance Signal Signal in exercise / energy-metabolism contexts. No performance claims unless sourced.
Illness / Stress Signal Signal in illness, vomiting, infection, or stress. Preserve caution; route to clinical boundary.
Diabetes-Associated Signal Signal in a diabetes / insulin / glucose context. Must not diagnose DKA or say a reader is safe.
Medication-Context Signal Signal where a medication changes interpretation. Careful sourcing and boundary language required.
Laboratory Measurement Signal Signal by specimen, assay, and reporting language. Keep blood, urine, and breath measurement separate.
DKA Concern Boundary Boundary class for a clinical concern frame. Not a diagnosis; links to care and sources.
Emergency Referral Boundary Boundary class for urgent-evaluation language. Not treatment advice; must not self-triage.
