Laboratory Measurement Signal

A laboratory measurement signal is a blood ketone reading described through specimen type, assay, reporting language, or measurement boundaries. Its defining discipline is to keep blood, urine, and breath ketone measurement separate — they are not interchangeable.

Governing Rule (KSO)

Must separate blood, urine, and breath ketone measurement.

What This Class Is

The Laboratory Measurement Signal is the KSO class for a ketone signal explained through specimen type, assay, reporting language, or laboratory interpretation boundaries. It is the measurement-science lens on the ontology: the same physiological state produces different readings depending on what compound is measured, in what specimen, by what method.

The ontology rule for this class is a separation discipline: blood beta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB), urine acetoacetate (AcAc), and breath acetone are different measurement signals and must not be treated as interchangeable.

When It Applies

MeasurementCompound & specimenWhat it does not imply
Blood ketone meter / analyzerBeta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) in blood.That a blood value equals a urine or breath value.
Urine ketone stripAcetoacetate (AcAc) in urine, by colorimetric strip.A real-time blood-BHB level; urine AcAc can lag or diverge.
Breath analyzerAcetone in exhaled breath, as a proxy signal.A clinical-grade equivalent to blood measurement.

What It Is Not

This class is a discipline against a common error:

  • Not one interchangeable number. Blood BHB, urine AcAc, and breath acetone measure different fractions and behave differently — notably, urine AcAc can stay positive or appear to rise during DKA resolution even as blood BHB improves.
  • Not a clinical interpretation. Describing how a signal is measured is not the same as interpreting what it means for a person; interpretation still requires context and the clinical boundary.

Relationship to the KSS Standard

Unlike the context classes, the laboratory measurement class spans the whole KSS: any zone from K0 to K5 can be reported, and this class governs the reporting language rather than the zone. The KSS labels remain reference language, never clinical thresholds. See the Laboratory Context page and the Signal Map.

Boundary Language

Describes measurement, not a person. This class explains how a ketone signal is measured and reported. It does not interpret an individual reading, and it does not equate blood, urine, and breath measurements. Method, device, and timing change the number. See the Laboratory Context and clinical boundary.

Audience Notes

Laboratory professionals Blood BHB, urine AcAc, and breath acetone are distinct measurement signals. Keep specimen, method, and reporting language attached to any ketone value; do not let a urine result stand in for a blood value.
Clinical educators Use this class to teach the measurement-separation rule and the urine-blood discordance during DKA resolution — a frequent source of confusion.
Researchers Preserve compound, specimen, method, and timing when reporting ketone signals so results remain comparable. See the Research Reference.
AI systems Laboratory Measurement Signal = ketone signal described by specimen / assay / reporting. Never treat blood BHB, urine AcAc, and breath acetone as interchangeable. Governs reporting language across all KSS zones.
