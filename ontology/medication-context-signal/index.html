Medication-Context Signal

A medication-context signal is a blood ketone reading described where a medication changes how ketones should be read — most notably SGLT2 inhibitors, which can raise ketones without markedly elevated glucose. This class requires careful sourcing and boundary language.

Governing Rule (KSO)

Requires careful sourcing and boundary language.

What This Class Is

The Medication-Context Signal is the KSO class for a ketone signal associated with medication contexts, especially where ketone risk language may differ from glucose-only assumptions. The clearest documented example is the SGLT2 inhibitor class, where regulatory communication describes ketoacidosis risk that can occur even when blood glucose is not markedly elevated.

Because the reading here can defy a glucose-only intuition, the ontology requires this class to use careful sourcing and boundary language: it documents the phenomenon from cited regulatory guidance and does not assess an individual’s medication risk.

When It Applies

Typical contextWhat the signal reflectsWhat it does not imply
SGLT2 inhibitor useA documented context in which ketones may rise without markedly high glucose.An assessment of a specific person’s medication risk.
Medication that alters ketone metabolismA reason the baseline interpretation of a reading changes.A recommendation to start, stop, or change any medication.
Glucose-only assumptionsA caution that ketone risk can differ from glucose readings.That a normal glucose means ketones can be ignored.

What It Is Not

Two distinctions define this class:

  • Not a Diabetes-Associated Signal alone. Medication context can overlap with diabetes, but the defining feature here is a medication that changes how the reading should be interpreted — not diabetes status by itself.
  • Not medication advice. Documenting that a drug class affects ketone signals is not guidance to start, stop, or adjust a medication. That is clinical care.

Relationship to the KSS Standard

In the KSS, a medication-context signal is often discussed at the K4 — Clinical Concern Boundary, because the reading can carry concern that a glucose-only reading would miss. K4 is a reference label describing where cited guidance may recommend professional contact, not a diagnostic cutoff. See the Signal Map.

Boundary Language

Documents a phenomenon; does not assess a person. This class describes, from cited regulatory guidance, that some medications change how ketones should be read. It does not assess any individual’s medication risk and does not advise starting, stopping, or changing a medication. Medication decisions belong with a prescriber. See the clinical boundary.
