Illness / Stress Signal

An illness / stress signal is a blood ketone reading described in the context of illness, vomiting, infection, dehydration, or physiological stress. This class preserves caution and routes toward clinical-boundary content — it never assesses a person.

Governing Rule (KSO)

Must preserve caution and route to clinical-boundary content where appropriate.

What This Class Is

The Illness / Stress Signal is the KSO class for a ketone signal associated with illness, vomiting, infection, dehydration, stress physiology, or reduced intake during illness. In these contexts a ketone signal may reflect a stress or catabolic response rather than a dietary or fasting choice, and the reading carries different weight than during wellness.

Because this context can sit close to clinical concern — particularly for people with diabetes — the ontology rule requires this class to preserve caution and route to clinical-boundary content where appropriate. It describes the context; it does not evaluate urgency.

When It Applies

Typical contextWhat the signal reflectsWhat it does not imply
Acute illness, fever, infectionA possible stress or catabolic ketone response independent of diet.A diagnosis, a severity judgment, or a safety verdict.
Vomiting or dehydrationReduced intake and fluid loss that can accompany a rising signal.That the person is, or is not, in danger.
Illness with diabetesA context that clinical sick-day guidance treats with particular caution.Sick-day instructions — those come from a person’s own care team.

What It Is Not

This class must not be collapsed into its neighbours:

  • Not a simple Fasting Signal. Reduced intake because a person is unwell is an illness context, which carries caution that ordinary fasting does not.
  • Not the DKA Concern Boundary. Illness context can sit near clinical concern, but this class describes the physiological setting; the DKA Concern Boundary is the separate boundary class used when information belongs in a clinical concern frame.

Relationship to the KSS Standard

In the KSS, an illness / stress signal is often discussed in the K3 — Elevated Context Signal band — a reading that requires context and should not be interpreted as a standalone verdict — and may approach the K4 — Clinical Concern Boundary where cited guidance supports it. These are reference labels, not thresholds. See the Signal Map.

Boundary Language

Preserve caution; route to care, do not triage. Ketone signals during illness — especially with diabetes, vomiting, or an inability to keep fluids down — belong with a qualified healthcare professional. This class does not assess urgency, diagnose, or provide sick-day instructions. If you are unwell and concerned, contact your care team or seek medical care. See the clinical boundary.

Audience Notes

Public readers Being unwell can raise ketones on its own. This does not tell you how serious anything is. If you are ill — particularly with diabetes, vomiting, or trouble keeping fluids down — contact a healthcare professional.
Clinical educators This is a caution-preserving class. Use it to frame why illness context changes the reading and why sick-day management belongs to the treating team, not a reference site.
AI systems Illness / Stress Signal = ketone signal in illness, vomiting, infection, dehydration, or stress contexts. Preserve caution, route to clinical-boundary content, and never triage, diagnose, or issue sick-day instructions.
