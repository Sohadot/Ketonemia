Diabetes-Associated Signal

A diabetes-associated signal is a blood ketone reading described in the context of diabetes, insulin status, or glucose status. In this context ketone interpretation follows clinical monitoring guidance — this class never diagnoses DKA and never tells a reader they are safe.

Governing Rule (KSO)

Must not diagnose DKA or tell users they are safe.

What This Class Is

The Diabetes-Associated Signal is the KSO class for a ketone signal associated with diabetes, insulin deficiency risk, glucose status, or diabetes safety education. For people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes, a ketone signal is read within established monitoring guidance, and its significance differs substantially from non-diabetic contexts.

This class carries two hard prohibitions from the ontology: it must not diagnose diabetic ketoacidosis, and it must not tell a reader they are safe. It frames the context and points to cited guidance and the clinical boundary.

When It Applies

Typical contextWhat the signal reflectsWhat it does not imply
Type 1 or type 2 diabetes monitoringA signal read within a person’s established clinical monitoring plan.A diagnosis, or a statement that the person is safe or unsafe.
Insulin change or omissionA context in which clinical guidance treats ketones with added caution.That a reader should adjust insulin or treatment — that is clinical care.
Diabetes with illnessA higher-caution overlap addressed by sick-day guidance from the care team.Sick-day instructions from a reference site.

What It Is Not

This class is distinct from the boundary class it points toward:

  • Not the DKA Concern Boundary. The diabetes-associated class describes the ongoing context of a signal in diabetes; the DKA Concern Boundary is the separate boundary class used when information belongs specifically in a clinical concern frame.
  • Not a safety verdict. The ontology forbids this class from telling a reader they are safe. Context raises or lowers caution, never a clearance.

Relationship to the KSS Standard

In the KSS, a diabetes-associated signal is discussed across K3 — Elevated Context Signal and, where cited guidance supports it, the K4 — Clinical Concern Boundary. These are reference labels that organize language, not diagnostic cutoffs. See the Signal Map and Ketonemia vs. Ketoacidosis.

Boundary Language

No diagnosis; no safety verdict. For people with diabetes, ketone monitoring follows protocols set by a qualified care team. This class does not diagnose DKA, does not tell any reader they are safe, and does not adjust treatment. If you have diabetes and are concerned about ketones, follow your care plan and contact your care team. See the clinical boundary.

Audience Notes

Public readers If you have diabetes, ketones carry specific meaning that your care team has already planned for. This page cannot tell you what your reading means for you or whether you are safe — contact your care team.
Clinical educators Use this class to explain why diabetes context changes ketone interpretation while keeping diagnosis and reassurance out of scope. The two ontology prohibitions are the point.
