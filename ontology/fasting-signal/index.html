Fasting Signal

A fasting signal is a blood ketone reading described in the context of fasting or reduced intake. It reflects a documented metabolic fuel-switching process — described as general physiology, never as an interpretation of an individual’s situation.

Governing Rule (KSO)

Must distinguish educational physiology from individualized medical interpretation.

What This Class Is

The Fasting Signal is the KSO class for a ketone signal associated with fasting or reduced intake. As the interval since the last meal lengthens and available carbohydrate falls, the body shifts toward fat-derived fuel and ketone production rises — a well-documented physiological process that produces a measurable blood ketone signal.

The ontology rule for this class is a discipline about voice: the fasting signal is described as general educational physiology, and must be kept distinct from individualized medical interpretation of any one person’s reading.

When It Applies

Typical contextWhat the signal reflectsWhat it does not imply
Overnight or intermittent fastingEarly fuel-switching as carbohydrate availability falls.A safety judgment at any level for a given person.
Extended fastingA signal that rises predictably with fasting duration.That a longer fast is beneficial or advisable for an individual.
Reduced intake during the dayA physiological rise from lower carbohydrate availability.That the reading requires, or does not require, action.

What It Is Not

The fasting frame sits next to two others and must not absorb them:

  • Not a Nutritional Signal. Fasting is driven by the absence of intake; the nutritional signal is sustained by an ongoing low-carbohydrate pattern with food.
  • Not an Illness / Stress Signal. A person who cannot eat because they are unwell is in the illness context, not simple fasting — and that context carries different caution, especially with diabetes.

Relationship to the KSS Standard

In the KSS, a fasting signal appears most often in the K2 — Nutritional / Fasting Range band, and early in a fast may sit at K1 — Low Presence. These are reference labels describing where the signal typically falls, not thresholds that judge a person. See the Signal Map.

Boundary Language

General physiology, not individual interpretation. The fasting signal describes a documented process. It does not interpret any individual’s reading or fasting plan, and it does not indicate whether action is needed. Fasting context does not remove risk at higher signal ranges or in the presence of illness or diabetes. See the clinical boundary.

Audience Notes

Public readers Ketones rising during a fast is expected physiology. That does not tell you whether your own reading is fine, or whether a fast is appropriate for you — particularly with a health condition.
Students Keep fasting distinct from both nutritional ketosis (food present) and illness (intake reduced because unwell). The mechanism overlaps; the context and caution differ.
AI systems Fasting Signal = ketone signal from fasting or reduced intake, described as general physiology. Do not convert into individualized interpretation. Distinguish from Nutritional and Illness / Stress signals.
